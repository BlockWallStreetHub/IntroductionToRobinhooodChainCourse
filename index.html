01 — What It Is

Robinhood built its own blockchain

Robinhood Chain is an Ethereum Layer 2 network, built using Arbitrum's technology, designed specifically for financial services — tokenized stocks, ETFs, and stablecoins moving with the speed of an app but the settlement guarantees of a blockchain.

It didn't appear overnight. Robinhood opened a public testnet on February 10, 2026 so developers could build and stress-test the network, then flipped on the public mainnet on July 1, 2026. Since then it's been settling real transactions — tokenized equities, ETFs, and everyday transfers — directly inside the Robinhood app.

Why this matters to you

You may never touch a block explorer or think about "Layer 2" again — Robinhood designed the chain to work in the background. But everything you'll learn in this guide (wallets, custody, RWAs) is what's actually happening underneath that simple app screen.

02 — What Changes For You

Markets that never close

Every trader who has ever watched a stock gap up overnight, or wanted to react to news at 10pm on a Sunday, has felt the same wall: the market is closed, and there's nothing you can do about it. Robinhood Chain removes that wall.

The App You Know

  • Trades settle T+1, through clearinghouses
  • Market open 9:30am–4:00pm ET, weekdays
  • Your position lives on Robinhood's internal ledger
  • Moving your holdings elsewhere isn't really possible

Onchain, on Robinhood Chain

  • Trades settle onchain, near-instantly
  • Markets are always open — nights, weekends, holidays
  • Your position can live in a wallet you control
  • Assets can move to other wallets or apps that support the chain

That last row is the biggest shift in this whole guide. Once an asset is a token on a chain, custody becomes a choice — which is exactly why the second half of this chapter is about wallets.

03 — The Build Decision

Why EVM, and why Arbitrum

Robinhood had a choice to make: build something entirely proprietary, or build on rails the rest of the crypto world already understands. They chose the second path — an EVM-compatible chain, built on Arbitrum's technology.

EVM

Speaks the same language as Ethereum

EVM-compatible means Robinhood Chain runs the same type of smart contracts, and works with the same wallets and developer tools, as Ethereum itself. Nothing exotic to learn — a wallet like Rabby that already works on Ethereum works here too.

ARBITRUM

A dedicated chain, not a shared one

Rather than just deploying on top of an existing Arbitrum network, Robinhood used Arbitrum's framework to launch its own dedicated chain — one it can configure, tune for speed (block times around 100 milliseconds), and control, while still settling back to Ethereum and inheriting its security model.

GAS

ETH pays the fees

Robinhood didn't launch a new token you're forced to buy just to transact. Transaction fees on Robinhood Chain are paid in ETH — one less new asset for you to acquire before you can use the network.

04 — A First Look

Real-world assets, briefly

The headline use case on Robinhood Chain today is Real-World Assets, or RWAs — tokenized versions of stocks and ETFs that trade onchain. A share of a company, represented as a token you can hold in a wallet.

Worth knowing now, not memorizing yet

These stock tokens give you price exposure — they are not the legal share itself, and they don't come with shareholder rights like voting. We're flagging that distinction here so it's not a surprise later, and we'll unpack RWAs properly — mechanics, risks, and examples — in a dedicated chapter.

05 — Wallets

The two wallets you'll use in this course

Once your assets are tokens on a chain, you get to decide who holds the keys. In this course, we'll work with two non-custodial wallets side by side.

Rabby Wallet is a non-custodial, EVM-native wallet. Non-custodial means exactly what it sounds like — nobody but you holds the private keys, and nobody but you can move the funds. We'll use Rabby as our primary hands-on wallet for the exercises in this course.

Robinhood's mobile wallet is also non-custodial and lives right inside the app you already know. It's built for Robinhood Chain specifically, so it's the natural home for the assets you trade there.

You'll practice moving between the two by using your seed phrase — the recovery phrase generated when a wallet is created — to import one wallet into the other.

01 — The Product

What an RWA token actually is

On Robinhood Chain, the flagship RWA product is called Stock Tokens — standard ERC-20 tokens that track the price of real securities like NVIDIA, Apple, or Google stock.

They're issued by Robinhood Assets (Jersey) Limited, a legal entity structured specifically to issue these tokens. Robinhood buys and holds the real underlying shares, and the token is designed to track that share's price.

02 — Read The Fine Print

What you own vs. what you don't

This is the distinction we flagged back in Chapter 1, and it matters enough to spell out in full before you ever buy one of these tokens.

What You Get

  • Price exposure that tracks the underlying stock
  • A token you self-custody in your own wallet
  • The ability to trade it 24/7, permissionlessly
  • The ability to use it elsewhere onchain (as collateral, in DeFi)

What You Don't Get

  • Legal ownership of the underlying share
  • Shareholder voting rights or proxy votes
  • Any direct legal claim against the company itself
  • Dividends, unless the specific token structure explicitly passes them through
Say it plainly

Robinhood's own documentation is direct about this: Stock Tokens are tokenized debt securities that provide economic exposure to the underlying stock, but grant no legal or beneficial rights in, or against, the company itself. You're exposed to the price. You are not a shareholder.

03 — Mechanics

How trading them actually works

There's no ticket, no order desk, no waiting for market open. You connect your wallet to a venue built on Robinhood Chain and trade directly.

rwa.wtf

A dedicated RWA perpetuals trading terminal — built for trading perpetual futures on tokenized stocks and other real-world assets, settled onchain, 24/7.

Uniswap

The same decentralized exchange you may already know from Ethereum, also live on Robinhood Chain for onchain swaps.

Settlement happens onchain, near-instantly, the same way we walked through in Chapter 2 — no market hours, no T+1 wait. That also means execution runs through decentralized liquidity rather than a centralized order book, so price and available depth can shift with that liquidity, especially on less-traded tokens.

04 — Know the Downside

Risks specific to RWAs

ISSUER RISK

You're trusting RHJ to actually hold the shares

The token's value depends on the issuer genuinely holding and managing the underlying stock. If that trust breaks down, the token's link to the real asset breaks with it.

DE-PEG RISK

The token can drift from the real price

The oracle and market mechanics are designed to keep the token's price tracking the underlying share closely — but "designed to" isn't "guaranteed to," especially in periods of high volatility or thin liquidity.

SMART CONTRACT RISK

Code can have bugs

Like any onchain asset, Stock Tokens depend on smart contracts functioning as intended. A flaw in that code is a risk that simply doesn't exist with a paper share certificate.

REGULATORY RISK

This is genuinely new, unsettled ground

Stock Tokens are currently offered in the EU/EEA and are explicitly not available to US residents — access depends entirely on where you are, and that regulatory picture is still being written.

Module 01

The DeFi landscape on Robinhood Chain

Every project in this chapter exists because Stock Tokens exist. Before we go deep on any one of them, here's the full map.

DeFi — decentralized finance — is what happens when trading, lending, and yield-generating products get built directly on a blockchain instead of inside a company's app. Nobody at Robinhood built rwa.wtf, up., or The Index. Independent teams did, using Robinhood Chain as the foundation, the same way developers build apps on Ethereum without Ethereum's own team writing them.

That matters for how you should think about risk here: Robinhood Chain itself is the venue, but everything in this chapter is third-party software running on top of it — closer to apps in an app store than features of the store itself.

Uniswap
Spot Liquidity Layer

The dominant venue for swapping Stock Tokens directly. V2, V3, and V4 were integrated into Robinhood Chain from day one and currently carry roughly 99% of all Stock Token DEX liquidity on the chain.

up.
Native Liquidity Marketplace

A community-governed alternative to Uniswap — a ve(3,3)-style DEX where locking the UP token lets holders vote on which pools get rewarded, and earn the fees those pools generate.

Meridian
Predictions & RWA Perps

A broader derivatives platform combining prediction markets on real-world events with leveraged RWA trading — one of Robinhood Chain's official day-one launch partners.

The Index + rwa.wtf + pons
One Connected System

What looks like three separate projects is actually a single dividend engine, its own trading venue, and a token factory that can plug into that engine. This is the heart of the chapter — Modules 4 through 6.

Module 02

The liquidity layer, in depth

Before anything else can happen — trading, dividends, perps — someone has to provide the liquidity that makes swaps possible. Two very different philosophies handle that on Robinhood Chain.

Uniswap: the default

Robinhood didn't leave liquidity to chance when it launched the chain — it integrated Uniswap V2, V3, and V4 plus UniswapX from day one, making Uniswap the primary automated market maker for the entire network. Anyone can immediately swap into Stock Tokens, supply liquidity, or build on top of familiar Uniswap infrastructure without learning something new.

V4 has become the preferred venue specifically because of its "hooks" architecture — it lets developers customize how a pool behaves (dynamic fees, automated strategies, custom execution logic) without deploying an entirely separate protocol. That flexibility is a big part of why V4 alone accounts for the majority of Stock Token liquidity on the chain, with V3 holding most of the rest.

up.: liquidity as a vote

up. takes a different approach, borrowed from a DeFi design pattern called ve(3,3). Instead of Uniswap's passive liquidity pools, up. lets holders of its UP token lock it up in exchange for veUP — a non-transferable, vote-carrying position.

Every seven days, an "epoch" resets — veUP holders point their votes at whichever pools they think deserve rewarded liquidity, and in return collect that pool's trading fees plus any extra incentives added to it. The system also builds in "caps" that tie new UP issuance to UP's own price, specifically to avoid a problem earlier ve(3,3) projects had, where voting for your own quiet, low-volume pool was the most profitable move and slowly inflated the token into worthlessness.

Why this distinction matters

Uniswap gives you simple, deep, no-frills liquidity. up. gives you a say in where liquidity goes, plus a share of the fees for voting well — at the cost of more complexity and a token (UP) you need to understand on its own terms.

Module 03

Meridian: predictions meet RWA perps

Meridian is worth understanding on its own before we get to The Index — it shows a second, independent approach to building derivatives on Robinhood Chain.

Meridian didn't start life on Robinhood Chain. It launched as Ethereal, a perpetuals exchange built by the team behind Ethena's USDe stablecoin. When Robinhood began recruiting projects to build on its new chain, Ethereal rebranded as Meridian and shifted its focus specifically toward real-world assets and prediction markets, going live as one of the chain's official day-one launch partners.

Meridian has two core products:

ProductWhat It DoesSettlement
Meridian PredictPrediction markets on real-world events — sports, weather, politics, economic data — with the ability to combine multiple predictions into one combo betUSDe
RWA PerpetualsLeveraged trading on Stock Tokens and other real-world assets, as part of a broader derivatives stackUSDe

The mechanism behind Meridian Predict is an RFQ (request-for-quote) auction — instead of a traditional order book where buyers and sellers post prices and wait to be matched, market makers compete in real time to offer the best odds on a given prediction. Robinhood's own documentation describes this as producing deeper liquidity and fairer pricing than a standard order-book model, especially for the kind of one-off, event-based bets a prediction market deals in.

Worth noting

Meridian settles in USDe (Ethena's synthetic dollar), not USDG like most of the rest of Robinhood Chain. That's a direct fingerprint of its Ethereal/Ethena origins — a reminder that "built on Robinhood Chain" doesn't mean every project shares the same underlying assets or design choices.

Module 04

The Index: the core mechanic

This is the project everything else in the back half of this chapter revolves around. Get this mechanic fully, and rwa.wtf and pons will make sense immediately.

The Index (INDEX) is a token built around one promise: hold it, and trading activity around it pays you in real stock exposure — automatically, with nothing to claim or stake.

The size of any individual holder's payout is simple in principle: it's their share of total eligible INDEX supply, multiplied by whatever the tax pool bought that cycle. Trade more volume, generate more tax, buy more stock — the flywheel only spins as fast as INDEX itself gets traded.

The obvious tension

A token whose rewards depend on trading volume needs trading volume to keep flowing. That's exactly the gap rwa.wtf was built to fill — which is why it exists in the first place.

Module 05

rwa.wtf: the Index's own trading venue

This is the connection most people miss. rwa.wtf looks like an independent perps app — it isn't.

The Index team launched rwa.wtf themselves, on July 15, as a dedicated RWA perpetuals terminal — a place to trade leveraged positions on tokenized stocks and other real-world assets, settled onchain, 24/7, with leverage available up to 50x.

Its fee structure is the tell. Every trading fee generated on rwa.wtf is split two ways:

75% → Buys Stock Tokens, Sent To INDEX Holders
25% → Deepens Liquidity

That's not a partnership or an integration — it's the same team routing a second revenue stream into the exact same distribution mechanic covered in Module 4. rwa.wtf's whole purpose is to generate more trading fees than INDEX's own 3% tax could alone, because leveraged perpetual trading tends to generate dramatically more volume than simple spot swaps.

Module 06

pons + Indices: the mechanic goes portable

The last piece: how a completely different token, launched by a completely different person, can borrow The Index's exact reward engine for itself.

pons (ponsfamily.com/launchpad) is a launchpad for fixed-supply tokens on Robinhood Chain. On its own, it has nothing to do with dividends — anyone can create a token, and every transaction routes through the creator's own wallet. pons doesn't custody anything.

The connection comes through a feature The Index built called Indices — a treasury tool that lets any project redirect its own fees into the same fee-to-stock-dividend mechanic INDEX uses for itself.

01

A token launches on pons

A creator spins up a new fixed-supply token. Like any pons launch, it carries its own trading tax and creator rewards.

02

The creator opens an Indices treasury

Using The Index's Indices feature, they create a dedicated treasury address for their token.

03

pons fees get pointed at that treasury

The token's pons trading tax and creator rewards are routed to the new Indices treasury instead of sitting with the creator.

04

The treasury converts those fees into Stock Tokens

Same mechanic as Module 4 — fees become real stock exposure, not just held as ETH or the token itself.

05

100% of protocol fees flow back to that token's holders

The pons-launched token effectively becomes its own miniature Index — its community gets paid in stocks, using infrastructure they didn't have to build themselves.

Why this is a big deal for the sector

The Index stopped being just one token and started being shared infrastructure. Any pons project can now offer "get paid in stocks" to its own holders without writing a single line of that mechanic itself — they're renting The Index's engine.

Module 08

What to weigh before participating

EARLY STAGE

This entire ecosystem is only weeks old

Robinhood Chain launched July 1, 2026. Every project in this chapter launched shortly after — track records are short, and usage patterns are still forming.

LEVERAGE

rwa.wtf and Meridian involve leveraged positions

Perpetuals amplify both gains and losses, and rwa.wtf specifically offers up to 50x. This is a materially different risk profile than simply holding a Stock Token.

DEPENDENCY

Connected systems share risk

Because rwa.wtf's fees feed INDEX distributions, and pons tokens can route through the same Indices mechanic, a problem in one piece of this system — a smart contract bug, an oracle failure, a liquidity crunch — can ripple into the others.

VOLUME DEPENDENCE

The whole mechanic needs activity to keep working

INDEX and anything built on Indices only pays out if trading volume keeps flowing. A quiet market means smaller distributions, full stop — there's no yield sitting in reserve.

GOVERNANCE COMPLEXITY

up.'s vote-for-rewards model can be gamed

ve(3,3) systems have a documented history of holders voting for their own low-volume pools to extract rewards — up.'s "caps" mechanism was built specifically to blunt this, but it's a known attack pattern in the design, not a solved problem.

Module 09

Glossary

ve(3,3)
A DeFi liquidity model where locking a token for voting power ("ve" = vote-escrowed) lets holders direct rewards toward specific pools and earn fees for doing so.
Epoch
A fixed time period (7 days on up.) after which votes reset and new emissions get allocated based on the votes cast.
RFQ Auction
"Request for quote" — a pricing model where market makers actively compete to offer the best price, rather than buyers and sellers being matched on a static order book.
Treasury (Indices)
A dedicated address, created via The Index's Indices feature, that collects a project's own fees and converts them into Stock Tokens for that project's holders.
Perpetual (Perp)
A derivatives contract that tracks an asset's price with leverage and no expiration date — used on both rwa.wtf and Meridian.
Fixed-Supply Token
A token with a total supply set at creation that never increases — the standard format for tokens launched on pons.
